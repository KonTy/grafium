Use **Journal** when the date is the natural place to begin. Write a thought now; link it to a lasting topic when the connection becomes clear.

## Go to a date—not just today

Open **Journal**, then **Go to date** to open the calendar. Choose a day in the past to revisit or backfill a note, or a day in the future to prepare a daily page.

Use the calendar's **Previous** and **Next** arrows to browse months. For a bigger jump:

1. Click the **year heading** to open a 12-year grid; use its arrows to browse more years.
2. Choose a year, then a month, then a day. The **month heading** also opens the 12-month picker directly.
3. Use **Today** to jump straight back to the present.

Picking a past or future date opens that day's journal, creating it if needed. Entries keep their normal chronological order; older days also scroll below the current day. Choose dates with the calendar controls—there is no typed-date field.

Try **July 20, 1969**: this graph includes [[1969-07-20]], a clearly labeled historical example. It is not a personal diary entry or an assigned deadline.

## Journal shortcuts and a different kind of diary

**Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+J** opens today's journal for editing. Use **g n** and **g p**, or **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+.** and **Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+,**, for the next and previous journal.

**Alt+D does not insert a date.** It inserts a link to [[personal/diary]], an ordinary page separate from dated journals. This graph includes a small example so the shortcut has somewhere useful to go. **Alt+T** inserts the current time.

For example, record a decision in [[Projects/Observatory Night/Log]] and connect the day's note to the project. For scheduling a task rather than opening a daily page, see [[Projects/Tasks And Dates]]. These calendars navigate journals and set task dates; they are not an external calendar integration.

On long journal views, older editors load as they approach the viewport and remain mounted once opened, helping preserve your active editing state while keeping initial loading light.
